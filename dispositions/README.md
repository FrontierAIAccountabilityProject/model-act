# Dispositions — the register of review outcomes

No disposition has been received. This page exists **before** the first one so that the rules
of publication are fixed in advance, in public, and cannot be bent around any particular
outcome. When a disposition arrives, it enters this folder under the rules below, in the same
commit as its changelog entry.

## The word, and why this project uses it

**A disposition is a reviewer's determination of a question, published under their own name or
anonymously at their election.** The word is borrowed from its judicial sense, where a
disposition is how a matter is finally determined rather than merely discussed. That is the
sense meant here: the reviewer disposes of the question they took, and the maintainer may
respond beside it but may not overrule or edit it.

The term is deliberate, and so is what it excludes. A disposition is not advice to the drafter,
not a consultation, not a comment the maintainer may weigh and set aside, and not an
endorsement of anything. **No disposition endorses this Act, and none ever will**; a reviewer
who concludes the central theory should be abandoned is published saying exactly that, with the
same prominence as any approval.

**The nearest familiar analogy is a conference paper rather than peer review.** A reviewer takes
a seat, works through the material in their lane, and writes a short paper of their findings,
which is then published as their work. Peer review serves the author by improving a manuscript
before publication; a disposition serves the reader, by putting an expert's determination on the
public record beside the text it examined, whatever it says.

## The aim

**The aim is not approval. It is a text that has been attacked in public by people qualified to
attack it, with the results published whether or not they favour the Act.**

A model statute is a proposal about how the state's coercive power should be used, and the only
honest way to offer one is to let people who know the field try to break it first, in public,
where the attempt can be read. That is why the floor is three findings verified or refuted
rather than an overall verdict, why the scope is agreed in writing before work begins, and why
this register exists before the first disposition arrived.

The stated success condition follows: **a disposition that refutes one of this project's findings
is worth more to it than a full pass that agrees with everything.** A statute nobody competent has
tried to break is not a safe statute, it is an untested one, and the difference matters most to
the people who would live under it.

## How the drafting world names these roles, and where this project departs

*Checked against the Uniform Law Commission's own practice, 25 August 2026, so the vocabulary is a
deliberate choice rather than an invention.*

The ULC is the institution that produces model and uniform acts in the United States, and its roles
have settled names. A **reporter** is the expert hired to draft the act on a drafting committee's
direction. **Commissioners**, around 350 of them, are attorneys appointed by the states who serve as
volunteers without compensation, debate the text, propose amendments and vote. **Advisors and
participating observers** are solicited to assist every drafting committee and bring specialised
knowledge from outside. A draft is then considered section by section at no fewer than two annual
meetings before the Committee of the Whole votes.

Three things in that vocabulary are worth borrowing and one is worth refusing.

**Borrowed: the unpaid volunteer expert.** The ULC's commissioners are unpaid, and nobody regards
that as diminishing their work. This project's seats are unpaid for the same reason: an expert who
is paid by the drafter is answering a client, and the whole point is that they are not.

**Borrowed: the outside observer with specialised knowledge.** The ULC solicits observers precisely
because a drafting committee cannot see its own blind spots. That is this project's premise, with
one difference of degree: here the observers are the only review there is.

**Borrowed: section-by-section scrutiny before adoption.** The ULC will not adopt an act on a
general impression, and neither will this one. Hence findings on specific provisions rather than an
overall verdict.

**Refused: the vote, and with it the endorsement.** A ULC act is approved by a body that then
advocates its adoption. This project has no body, takes no vote, and seeks no approval. A reviewer
here does not join a committee that owns the text; they determine a question and publish, and the
text has no standing beyond the arguments that survive.

That refusal is why the outcome is called a *disposition* rather than a report to a committee, and
why the analogy offered to invited reviewers is a conference paper: work that carries its author's
name and stands on its own, not a contribution absorbed into a collective product that someone
else then campaigns for.

## The rules, fixed in advance

**Published as written.** A disposition is published verbatim — the reviewer's own words, in
full, including "approved with reservations" and including hostile. Editing is limited to
formatting for the page; not one word changes. A reviewer who finds the central theory wrong is
published saying so, with the same prominence as any approval.

**Dated and scoped.** Each disposition states its date, the lane reviewed, the exact text
version reviewed (tag and hash), and the scope the reviewer agreed in writing before work
began. A disposition speaks only to its scope; no file may cite it beyond that.

**Attribution is the reviewer's election.** A named council seat requires an expressly
attributable disposition; short of that, a reviewer may elect anonymity, and the election is
honoured everywhere and permanently. No name is used to imply more than the written disposition
says. Endorsement is never inferred — a disposition is a review outcome, not a signature on the
project.

**The maintainer's response is separate and labelled.** Where the maintainer answers a finding,
the answer appears below the disposition under its own heading, clearly the maintainer's, never
interleaved. Findings that change the text route through the cure queue and errata register,
cross-referenced from here.

**Nothing is deleted.** A superseded or withdrawn disposition is marked and kept, with the
history stated — the standing rule of the whole repository.

## Format of an entry

One file per disposition: `dispositions/<year>_<lane>_<n>.md` — header block (date · lane ·
text version reviewed · scope · attribution election), the disposition verbatim, then any
labelled maintainer response. This index gains a one-line row per entry.
