## How these are ordered, and why it is not the order in the table

**Not by how interesting they are, and not by the debt count.** By **what breaks if the reading goes
against us** — which is what the table's "Cited at" column already records and nothing has ever
sorted on.

*Kentucky v. Dennison* and *Global-Tech* carry identical weight in the debt figure. One is an 1861
case, overruled in relevant part, that nothing leans on. The other is the constitutional ceiling on
the willful-blindness theory SEC. 6(b) depends on. **Reading them in table order would be a mistake.**

**1. *Global-Tech Appliances v. SEB S.A.*, 563 U.S. 754 (2011)** — glossary; the willful-blindness ceiling
`RECORD_SCOTUS_Global-Tech-v-SEB_563-US-754_2011_govinfo-US-Reports.pdf`
✅ **govinfo U.S. Reports print — it can settle its own pincites.**

## What to produce, per document

**Not a summary.** For each:

1. **Quote in hand** — the sentence the repository relies on, verbatim, with the page **if and only
   if the copy carries real pagination**. A slip opinion carries slip pages; a law.resource.org
   capture carries none ([E47](../ledger/errata.md)).
2. **The table row rewritten** — ⚠ to ✅, with the quotation, the pincite or an explicit statement
   that it cannot be settled from this copy, and the limit named.
3. **Anything the repository has wrong**, as an erratum. Today produced eleven. The register is the
   product, not an embarrassment.
4. **Nothing else.** Do not summarize the case, do not add commentary, do not open new questions in
   other files.

## Four traps that have each cost a day

- **A grep returning nothing is evidence about the grep.** Extracted text has furniture inserted into
  it: page numbers land mid-sentence, words hyphenate across line breaks, quotation marks curl. Three
  false negatives on 26 August alone. Use `check_quotations.py --find "..."`, which normalizes.
- **Read the sentence after the one you are quoting.** A proposition a court states in order to
  reject it reads exactly like one it holds ([E64](../ledger/errata.md)).
- **A case number is not a document.** One matter can produce several opinions
  ([E59](../ledger/errata.md)).
- **⚠ is a read-status, never emphasis.** Using it decoratively inflates a published figure.

**And read the register before opening a question.** More than once a "finding" had already been made
days earlier ([E49](../ledger/errata.md)).
