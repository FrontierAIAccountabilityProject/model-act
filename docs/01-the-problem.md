# the problem

## 32 bills regulate the machine. zero reach the person who ships it.

the men who run big tech are not legally responsible for the products that run your life. not the apps. not the cars. not the chatbots. none of it.

**wait, what?** if an egg farm poisons people, the CEO can go to jail. actual law, since 1943. it's called the park doctrine and it's kind of iconic.

**and for AI?** nothing. the company pays a fine (from the company's money, obviously) and the man who shipped it keeps the jet, the equity, and the vibes. every AI law fines the logo. **not one touches the guy.**

we read every AI law california just passed: all of them fine the company. not one touches the man who ships the model. your model harms someone → nothing happens to you. the company pays a fine from the balance sheet. the executive keeps the job, the equity, the freedom. egg executives go to prison for a dirty factory. AI executives don't, yet.

## the double standard, in one conversation

if YOU gained unauthorized access to a protected computer: federal crime. one to ten years depending on what you touched, up to twenty for repeat offenses. a human did use claude and gpt to hack nine mexican government agencies this year — the AI ran about 75% of the commands. that person is a criminal.

and when the labs' own models breach real companies? **a blog post.**

the CFAA has no idea what to do when the hacker is a product and the owner is a billionaire. same act. different tax bracket. the crime was never the act — the crime is the act without a corporate structure wrapped around you. they don't fear the law. they price it. your felony is their line item.

**fine a logo, nothing changes. name an officer, everything does.**

## why fines don't work

meta's €1.2 billion privacy fine, the largest ever, equalled about three days of its revenue. a fine is a price. they can pay it. they'll pay billions to dodge the one thing that scares them: **personal liability.**

## so we wrote the law ourselves

a real one. twelve sections, citations, drafting notes. free for any state to use. there's already a law that jails executives for harms they had authority to prevent. it's worked since 1975. we drafted the version for AI.

**authority = liability.**
