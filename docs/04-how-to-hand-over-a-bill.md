# how to hand over a bill

## you can hand a lawmaker a finished bill.

this is not a loophole. it is how most laws start. lawmakers don't write most laws themselves. someone hands them a ready-written draft and asks them to introduce it. companies do it constantly: one investigation found 10,000+ copied model bills introduced in eight years. you're allowed to do it too.

**why a state, and not congress?** criminal law is the states' oldest job. no federal law makes an AI executive personally liable, and none blocks a state from doing it. state criminal law over conduct that harms people in-state is the piece washington reaches last.

**why one state is enough.** illinois passed one strange biometric privacy law in 2008. nobody watched. it quietly became the national standard, because it existed first. **one sponsor, one chamber, one state.**

**the bill in question.** a drafted state law making frontier AI executives personally criminally liable for safety failures, the way egg executives have been since 1943. public domain, citations checkable, and honest about being a working draft: page one lists what still needs local finishing, and whose job each piece is. that's normal — every handed-over bill gets redrafted by the state's own legislative counsel anyway. you hand over the architecture. they pour the concrete.

## who can do this: anyone who can send mail.

no citizenship, no residence, no freedom required. postage.

- **live in the state?** best case. your letter counts extra: offices weigh their own voters most.
- **live somewhere else? never lived there? not a citizen?** still fine. corporate lobbyists never lived in your district either. the draft is the credential.
- **american abroad?** you officially remain a constituent of your last US address. federal law. email from anywhere on earth.
- **in prison?** yes, you especially. the right to petition survives a sentence. in 1962, florida prisoner clarence gideon wrote a petition in pencil on prison paper. it became the rule that anyone accused who can't afford a lawyer gets one. the most consequential handed-over pages in american history came from a cell. a letter naming the bill and the website is enough: staff download what you can't attach.

**who receives it.** any of 7,000+ state lawmakers can introduce a bill. but bills live by who carries them. best doors: a lawmaker already writing AI bills; anyone on the committee that would hear it; the chair above all — chairs decide what gets a meeting. ask for "the legislative director."

## the honest odds

**the current market:** states see 200,000+ bills a year and pass about a quarter, five times congress's rate. thousands are handed over ready-written by corporations and industry groups: one investigation found 10,000+ copied model bills in eight years, 2,100 became law. ALEC alone lands ~200 laws a year. why lawmakers take them: thin staff, and a finished bill they can put their name on is a gift. the system already runs on handed-over text. we're just doing it in public, for free.

1. **free scales.** a lobbyist pitches one state for a fee. a public-domain bill knocks on 50 doors the same morning.
2. **lawmakers need bills.** every legislator must file something. freshmen arrive with empty portfolios. december is bill-hunting season. finished, cited text is a gift, not an ask.
3. **disasters shop for shelf-ready text.** food safety law passed because people died and a drafted bill already existed. the next AI incident sends a hundred staffers searching for ready language. there will be one bill waiting.
4. **three locals beat one expert.** three short emails from a lawmaker's own district outweigh one polished outside pitch. if you live in a target state, your email is worth more than ours.

**the goal was never 50 wins. it's one, anywhere, once.**

## the routine (4 minutes)

1. **step one** — the bill already exists. it's public domain, meaning free, meaning any lawmaker can take it, no credit needed: this repo.
2. **step two** — email any state lawmaker's office. yes, you're allowed. companies hand lawmakers ready-written bills literally thousands of times a year. it's how laws are made.
3. **step three, the script** — "hi! here is a bill idea: [the link]. it's a public-domain working draft, page one says what still needs finishing. i have some strongly worded opinions about why no law reaches the people who ship these models. thank you!"

that's it. that's the routine. one email. you don't need to be formal or finished. that email, sent this autumn, is the whole move.

**disagree with us? good. that's legislative input.** send congress YOUR take, any angle, end it: "add officer liability." ten good arguments beat a hundred copies.
