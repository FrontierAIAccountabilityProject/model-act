# the precedents

## who HAS gone to jail for a shipped product? the egg guy.

2015: an egg company CEO went to prison over a salmonella outbreak he didn't even know about. he had the authority to prevent it. that was enough.

it's called the park doctrine (*US v. Park*, 1975). it's been law since 1975. it just stops at the FDA's door. we drafted the law that extends it to AI execs.

**authority = liability. for eggs. so far.**

## it's true. meet elon.

2018: the SEC charged musk with securities fraud. he settled for $20 million, admitted nothing, and kept the CEO job. that fine was under 0.1% of his wealth at the time; today it's about 0.003%.

2025: a jury found autopilot partly caused a young woman's death. $243 million. tesla paid. **elon wasn't even a defendant.**

## zuck's name cost $4.9 billion to remove.

2019: the FTC drafted a complaint naming zuckerberg personally. shareholder suits allege facebook paid a $5 billion fine — about 50x what its own lawyers expected — to take his name off it. the fine landed the same day facebook reported $16.9 billion in quarterly revenue. four weeks of sales.

they'll pay billions to dodge the one thing that scares them: personal liability.

2026: the sequel. seven years after facebook allegedly paid $5 billion — 50x its own lawyers' estimate — to keep zuckerberg's name off an FTC complaint, four state AGs put him on a witness list. the oakland trial (opened 18 aug 2026, before the same judge who heard musk v. openai) demands up to $1.4 trillion — nearly the company's entire value — for designing products to addict minors while assuring parents they were safe. 29 states have sued; new mexico already won $942 million plus court-ordered design changes. and notice what even this reckoning cannot do: every dollar demanded is the company's. the founder testifies as a witness, not a defendant, because no statute reaches the person. that's not a gap in the outrage. it's a gap in the law. (washington post, 17 aug 2026; npr, cnbc, 17–18 aug 2026)

## the piracy receipts: same act, different tax bracket

**piracy is a crime. unless you do it at scale.** download one movie: fines, lawyers, maybe prison. download 7 million pirated books to build a chatbot: a settlement, a press release, and a bigger valuation.

- meta torrented ~82TB of pirated books from shadow libraries to train llama. internal messages: 'torrenting from a corporate laptop doesn't feel right.' they did it anyway.
- anthropic downloaded 7 million+ pirated books. a judge said the piracy was not fair use. consequences: a $1.5 billion settlement, about $3,000 per book, zero charges, business as usual.
- 2011: aaron swartz downloaded research articles from JSTOR. science. papers. he was charged with 13 felonies and faced up to 35 years in prison. he died by suicide in january 2013, aged 26, before trial. for one person, criminal copyright infringement still carries up to 5 years, per offense.

the crime was never the downloading. the crime is downloading without a corporate structure wrapped around you.

## and the tradition works

congress reached for exactly this in august 2026: house democrats calling for AI CEOs to answer questions under oath. it's a letter, not a subpoena, yet. but the grammar has changed. the oldest trick in the book, the one that worked on eggs in 1943.
