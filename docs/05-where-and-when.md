# where and when: the doors open in this order. some close.

why these states: they're the ones with lawmakers already writing AI law, and doors opening soonest. dates from official calendars. the lawmakers picking january's bills are picking them now, this autumn. wait for the "deadline" and you've already missed the choosing.

- **now, and this one's a real deadline · PENNSYLVANIA** — the session dies nov 30 by constitution; anything unfinished starts over. doors: pennycuick (wrote SAFECHAT), miller, saval. after nov 30, it refiles jan 5.
- **nov 9: door opens · TEXAS** — prefiling begins. not a cutoff, an opening: earliest in america, and texas only meets in odd years, so miss this cycle and the next is 2029. door: capriglione, wrote the first red-state AI law.
- **dec 7: door opens · CALIFORNIA** — the new session organizes at noon, bills drop day one. lawmakers are choosing their bills right now, this autumn. doors: mcnerney, bauer-kahan (the labs just killed her transparency bill; she has the grievance).
- **dec 7: door opens · WASHINGTON** — prefiling begins; any legislator can carry one.
- **jan 6 / jan 4-15 · NEW YORK / NEW MEXICO** — sessions convene. ny doors: gounardes, bores, the RAISE act authors. new mexico's 30-day sprint loves arriving-finished text.

**why not your state?** maybe it is. these six just have the nearest doors and the warmest hands. search "[your state] AI bill 2026," find the author, that's your door. no AI author? the consumer-protection or judiciary committee chair.

bills get chosen before doors "open," and the choosing is happening now.

# case study: pennsylvania

## how a bill actually moves (taught with a live one, SB 1090)

every date from the official record. this machine was invented here: independence hall, franklin's presses, dickinson's unsigned letters.

1. **anyone hands over text. nov 2025.** a person brings a problem, or finished draft language, to a senator's office. email, district office, hallway. the senator circulates a memo, public at palegis.us, and the reference bureau turns it into a numbered bill.
2. **a committee meeting you can watch. nov 18, 2025: 11-0.** a public meeting at the harrisburg capitol, streamed live at palegis.us, archived since 2019. most bills never get this meeting: the chair decides.
3. **every version is public, free, same day. feb 3, 2026.** amended and reissued as printer's no. 1423. every version, every vote by name, at palegis.us. no login, no fee.
4. **the money check, then the full vote. mar 17, 2026.** appropriations asks what it costs: 22-0. then the whole senate, on camera, roll call by name: 49-1.
5. **the walk across the rotunda. ← here since mar 18.** the bill is carried to the house and referred to its communications & technology committee. no meeting yet. that chair decides if it ever gets one. house returns sept 9.
6. **the governor's desk.** 10 days to sign or veto, 30 if the session has ended, and a post-adjournment veto cannot be overridden. he asked for this bill in his own budget address.

## what SB 1090 actually says (printer's no. 1423, the version that passed 49-1)

- **if a reasonable person would think the bot is human** — the operator must clearly say it isn't. (§3a)
- **if a user talks about suicide or self-harm** — the bot must route them to crisis services, and the operator must publish its protocol on its public website. qualified: "to the extent technologically feasible." (§3b)
- **if the operator knows, should suspect, or should have known the user is a minor** — disclose it's AI, remind them to take breaks at least every 3 hours, and take reasonable measures against sexually explicit material. constructive knowledge: you can't unknow your way out. (§3c)
- **who enforces, and how hard** — the attorney general only. civil penalty up to $10,000 per violation. no private right of action: a harmed family cannot sue under this act. california's twin lets harmed kids sue directly. (§5)

**and section 4, the sentence to read twice:** "this act shall not apply to the underlying artificial intelligence model unless the model is directly offered, configured or deployed as an AI companion." the app is regulated. the model beneath it is exempt, in black letter.

## the calendar is the weapon

every legislature runs on a clock. pennsylvania's is constitutional, and it is short.

- **sept 9, 2026 — the house returns.** first chance for SB 1090's committee meeting. also the window where new text can still enter and move.
- **sept 28, 2026 — the senate returns.** 1 p.m., streamed live. the last stretch of the two-year term begins.
- **nov 30, 2026 — everything unfinished dies.** the general assembly's term expires by law at midnight (101 pa. code §7.21). every bill not passed must start over from zero. and a bill reaching the governor in the final days becomes a "30-day bill": if he vetoes after adjournment, there is no override.
- **jan 5, 2027 — the resurrection.** the new two-year session convenes at noon. every idea refiles. this is the moment drafted text waiting in a co-sponsorship memo becomes a day-one bill.

**the civics lesson:** a bill the governor asked for, that passed one chamber 49-1, can still die of calendar. and the people who decide what refiles in january are choosing their bills this autumn. text handed over now is a statute's whole head start.

## pennsylvania invented this

the 1776 pennsylvania constitution, section 15, verbatim: *"all bills of public nature shall be printed for the consideration of the people, before they are read in general assembly the last time... and the reasons and motives for making such laws shall be fully and clearly expressed."*

print the bills for the people. state your reasons. publish every vote by name (§14). keep the presses free to examine the legislature (§35). pennsylvania's founding document *was* the check-our-work doctrine. citizens reading and handing over legal text is not a workaround here. it is the original design. and the tradition of finished text finding a sponsor is how model law has always worked: dickinson's unsigned letters, the uniform codes, the bills that cross state lines because they exist first.

**so here is ours.** SB 1090 stops at section 4: the underlying model is exempt, and no bill in harrisburg or anywhere reaches the officer who ships it. we drafted the one that does. public domain. cited. typeset. take the plates. the session that could file it convenes january 5.
