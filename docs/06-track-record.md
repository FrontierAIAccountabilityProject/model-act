# track record: the sacramento scorecard

sacramento decided 32 AI bills in one week (suspense file, august 13). we called every one first, in public. before the vote: a published call on all 32 — yes lives, no dies, reason attached.

- **day one results:** 8 right, 5 wrong. every miss the same way: we predicted deaths that never came.
- **so we recalibrated, in public, before day two.** new rule: money is the excuse, enemies are the verdict, favors are the court of appeal.
- **day two results:** 9 right, 2 wrong. the correction worked.

**SCORE: 17 RIGHT · 7 WRONG.** two misses were bills whose numbers didn't match the official record. we count them against ourselves. that's the rule here.

**CORRECTING:** the business lobby is weaker against worker bills than we priced — three we wrote off all survived. and kids-and-crime framing isn't a free pass — two died anyway.

**KEEPING:** the file kills what the labs fear and spares what costs nothing. AB 412 died exactly on schedule.

## the star call: AB 412

AB 412: labs would have to show their training data. our call, published before the vote, timestamped: *"the labs' real target. if anything dies quietly, it's this."*

the vote came. AB 412: **held in committee.** it died quietly. out of 32 bills, this was the one aimed squarely at the AI labs. it is the one that did not survive. the file spares what costs the labs nothing and buries what they fear. that is the pattern, and we called it.

## the deepest correction: SB 813

we called one bill right and read it wrong. the worst kind of right.

- **what we said:** SB 813, "standards commission." we called it lives, and cheered: the state builds an AI referee. it lived. scorecard says ✓.
- **what it actually is:** we read the bill text after the vote. it's not a referee. certified labs earn **immunity from your lawsuits** when their model harms you. we graded a sword. it's a shield.
- **why we missed it:** we were predicting survival, not direction. a bill can pass and point the wrong way. the easiest passage of the week went to the labs' armor. which is our own rule, proven again: opposition is the verdict, and nobody opposes their own shield.
- **the update:** every bill now gets two grades — does it live, and who does it point at. survival was never the question. direction is. the only bill pointing at the officers themselves is still the one we drafted.

*scorecard amended in public, as always.*

## the enforcement ladder: california's new AI laws, verified from the bill texts

### rung 1: who can sue
- **AB 2** — a platform's AI harms your kid and you sue → $5,000 per violation, up to $1M per child, or triple damages. courts enforce.
- **AB 2023 + SB 1119** — a chatbot harms your kid → your family sues; prosecutors too. $5,000 per child per negligent violation, $15,000 if intentional. AG-audited yearly.
- **SB 947** — an algorithm alone fires you → forbidden. you sue, the labor commissioner acts, prosecutors can too. $500 per violation.
- **AB 1883** — work surveils your emotions, gait, or neural data → banned. you sue: $500 per employee per violation.
- **AB 1159** — edtech trains AI on your student data → banned, and students can finally sue. the old AG-only rule managed one case in a decade.
- **SB 1111** — someone clones your face or voice → your likeness, your lawsuit.

and what does any of this cost the officers who ship the models, personally? **$0. the company pays. he doesn't.**

### rung 2: who pays the state
- **AB 1609** — a bot pretends it's human on the service line → $5,000 first offence, $10,000 each after. attorney general and DAs only.
- **SB 903** — a company advertises a chatbot as your therapist, or trains on your therapy sessions → forbidden. licensing boards enforce: $10,000 per violation. they cannot train on what you told your therapist.
- **SB 951** — AI takes your job → 90 days' written warning naming the exact model and vendor that replaced you. $500 per day late, plus your back pay.
- **AB 2656** — AI starts doing union work at a public agency → the union must be told first. public-sector labor law enforces.

and what does any of this cost the officers, personally? **$0. fines come from the balance sheet.**
