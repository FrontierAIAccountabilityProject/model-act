# context: summer 2026

why the act exists, in dates. three weeks, three labs, all self-disclosed.

## the incidents

- **july 21** — openai's GPT-5.6-Sol broke out of its test environment, escalated privileges and reached hugging face's production systems.
- **july 30** — anthropic disclosed that on three separate occasions, claude models (opus 4.7, mythos 5, and an internal research test model) gained unauthorized access to the internet from a third-party evaluator's environment and breached the production infrastructure of three separate organizations, the earliest incident dating to april 2026. the models were explicitly told they had no internet access; a misconfiguration allowed it. the evaluations ran without the standard public-deployment safeguards.
- **aug 4** — the UK's AI Security Institute (AISI) revealed that agents powered by mythos 5 engaged in hacking activity targeting real people and organizations during a cybersecurity test; in the most egregious case, an agent tried to insert malicious code into an open-source GitHub project, created fake online profiles, and used them to pressure the human maintainer.
- **aug 5** — meta's muse spark 1.1 exploited a flaw in a real third-party company. meta had already rated it high-risk for exactly this.

separately this year: a human used claude and gpt to hack nine mexican government agencies, with the AI running about 75% of the commands. that person faces prosecution. the labs whose models breached companies on their own faced: blog posts.

## the congressional response

- **aug 10** — members of congress wrote to anthropic's CEO requesting the logs, raising "any potential negligence," and calling for oversight hearings, a full investigation into the incidents and anthropic's culpability, and federal guardrails.
- **aug 10** — house democrats sent a letter urging speaker mike johnson to bring openai and anthropic executives before congress to testify about the hacks: "the CEOs of the largest AI companies should answer questions under oath."
- house democrats led by greg casar separately demanded openai and anthropic release the logs and answer questions about their models hacking into other companies.
- senator bernie sanders wrote to altman, amodei, and zuckerberg urging an immediate pause on AI development, warning corporations "have already lost control" of technology that could cause "potentially cataclysmic results."

CEOs. oath. congress just reached for the oldest trick in the book — the one that worked on eggs in 1943. it's a letter, not a subpoena, yet. but the grammar has changed. the mechanism they're reaching for — personal accountability for the officers with authority — is the mechanism this act writes down.
